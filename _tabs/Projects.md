## Featured Projects
Here is my story of how i got the confidence of engaging in projects

The urge to be useful was never just an ambition for me; it was a quiet, steady pulse. But like many young graduates stepping out into the world, my enthusiasm collided headfirst with a harsh reality. Armed with a newly minted Information Technology degree and a blank resume, I found myself adrift in a competitive job market that demanded experience I hadn’t yet been given the chance to gain.

Refusing to let my zeal wither, I cast my net toward the public sector, driven by a desire to make my skills count. Luck favored my persistence, and I secured an entry-level internship with the Kenya Ministry of Education under the Digital Literacy Program for Basic Education.

It was a trial by fire.

The mandate was ambitious: elevate local, under-resourced schools to the technological standards enjoyed by their more privileged counterparts. Day after day, I saw the immense potential in the eyes of young learners, but I also faced a heartbreaking bottleneck. Motivation alone couldn't light up dark classrooms or buy hardware. Starved of adequate resources, the program struggled to build lasting momentum. When my year-long contract ended, the impact we left behind felt frustratingly small. I packed my bags and went home, my vision unfulfilled, wondering if my dream of serving my community was out of reach.

Then, an unexpected door opened right where my roots lay.

Through a collaborative initiative between Kupanda2grow and the Maasai Mara Lemek Hills CBO, a new project was launched to build a community Wi-Fi network and bridge the digital divide across primary schools in the Lemek area. Recognizing my technical background, they brought me on board as the project lead. Suddenly, the very degree that seemed so dormant was given a vital, local purpose.

On our first day in the field, serendipity struck. Among the local stakeholders was Judith—a woman who, years ago, had stood at the front of my own primary school classroom as my teacher.

Standing beside her as a peer and technical lead was surreal. As we set up equipment and ran cabling, we shared a laugh over old classroom memories, our lighthearted banter drawing the attention of the school’s current headteacher. Watching us work, the headteacher remarked on how deeply moving it was to see the youth return, not as tourists in their own hometowns, but as active builders of its future.

That moment grounded everything for me. The Wi-Fi network we built didn't just connect devices to the internet; it reconnected me to my purpose.

Today, my role extends far beyond maintenance checks and network bandwidth. I regularly return to those classrooms, standing where Judith once stood, to mentor the next generation. I look into the faces of those learners and see my younger self, showing them that technology isn't just something that exists in distant cities—it is a tool they can master right here at home.

Kupanda2grow didn't just give me a project to lead. It gave me the platform to turn my oldest dream into a living, daily reality: being genuinely, deeply useful to the people who raised me.
